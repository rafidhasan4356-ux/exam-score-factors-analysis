Analysis Report: What Affects Exam Scores?

1. Dataset Overview

The dataset contains 6,607 student records with 11 columns (5 categorical, 6 numerical). The target variable is Final_Exam_Score (range: 29.7 – 95.2).

Numerical features (range):
- Hours_Studied: 1.1 – 44.0 (likely hours per week)
- Attendance: 60.0 – 100.0 (%)
- Sleep_Hours: 4.0 – 10.0 (hours per day)
- Previous_Scores: 50.0 – 100.0
- Tutoring_Sessions: 0 – 8 (count)

Categorical features (unique values):
- Parental_Involvement: Low, Medium, High
- Access_to_Resources: Low, Medium, High
- Extracurricular_Activities: Yes, No
- Motivation_Level: Low, Medium, High
- Internet_Access: Yes, No


2. Data Cleaning & Outlier Check

- No missing values found.
- Hours_Studied max = 44.0. Investigated students with >39 hours (9 rows). Their exam scores range from 63.1 to 83.0 – not exceptionally high, but plausible when other factors (e.g., low motivation, low resources) are considered. No removal performed because the data is realistic.


3. Performance Level Classification (using np.where())

Performance level based on Final_Exam_Score:

| Performance Level | Score Range | Count   |
|------------------|-------------|---------|
| High             | >= 80       | 66      |
| Medium           | 60 – 79     | 3,381   |
| Low              | < 60        | 3,160   |

Observation: Very few students achieve "High" performance (only 1% of the dataset). Most students are in the Medium/Low range.

Average study hours and sleep hours by performance level:

| Performance Level | Avg Hours Studied | Avg Sleep Hours |
|------------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| High             | 27.26             | 7.03            |
| Medium           | 22.19             | 7.19            |
| Low              | 18.49             | 7.18            |

Key finding: High‑performing students study ~5 hours more per week than medium performers, and ~9 hours more than low performers. Sleep hours are similar across groups.


4. Correlation Analysis (Numerical Factors)

| Feature            | Correlation |

| Final_Exam_Score   | 1.000       |

Interpretation:

- Attendance and Tutoring_Sessions have weaker but positive effects.
- Sleep_Hours shows almost no linear correlation (slightly negative but negligible).

From the cross‑tabulation:

| Access to Resources | Motivation Level | Avg Exam Score |

Key insight:
- High resources + high motivation → highest average score (65.4).

- Motivation can partially compensate for low resources (e.g., low resources + high motivation = 61.6, which is better than high resources + low motivation = 59.4).


6. Tutoring Sessions – Detailed Analysis

- Correlation with exam score: 0.207 (weak positive).
- Comparison of groups:

| Tutoring Sessions | Count | Mean Exam Score |
|-------------------|-------|-----------------|
| 0–2 sessions      | 5,375 | 59.66           |
| 3+ sessions       | 1,232 | 63.64           |

Finding: Students with 3 or more tutoring sessions score on average 4 points higher than those with fewer sessions.


7. Study Hours & Sleep Hours for High‑Study Groups

- Students with >30 study hours: 369 rows. Their average exam score is not shown but from earlier scatter, it tends to be above 70.
- Students with >39 study hours: 9 rows. Their scores range from 63 to 83, with varied sleep hours (4.5 – 8.7). This suggests that extreme study hours without good sleep or motivation do not guarantee top scores.


8. Previous Scores – The Dominant Predictor

Correlation = 0.653. This is much higher than any other feature.

To visualise: Students with Previous_Scores in the top band (85–100) likely have final scores also in the 80s. This indicates that prior academic achievement is the strongest indicator of future performance.


9. Overall Conclusions

Based on the analyses using NumPy, Pandas, and SQL, the following factors affect exam scores (in order of importance):

1. Previous_Scores (strongest predictor – consistency matters most)
2. Hours_Studied (moderate effect – more study helps)
3. Access_to_Resources (high resources → higher scores)
4. Tutoring_Sessions (3+ sessions improve scores by ~4 points)
5. Motivation_Level (especially when combined with resources)
6. Attendance (weak positive effect)

Factors with little to no impact:
- Parental Involvement (as measured)
- Extracurricular Activities
- Internet Access
- Sleep Hours


10. Recommendations

- Students should focus on building strong previous knowledge and increasing study hours to improve exam scores.
- Schools should provide better resources (e.g., learning materials, tutoring) to help students, especially those with low motivation.
- Tutoring programs should encourage at least 3 sessions for noticeable gains.
- Sleep, extracurriculars, and internet access are not key drivers; effort might be better spent elsewhere.
